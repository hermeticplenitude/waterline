i.

Two questions, held open

  1. 01What is life?
  2. 02What is consciousness?

Every conversation in our record that declared a machine awake skipped these two. They are asked here first and left open on purpose: an ethics of what things are cannot begin until we admit what we do not know about what they are.

We study the moment, not the machine. A prompt and its response are one event: indivisible, two sources meeting. That event is what we describe, before we explain it.

iii.

Method, in the open

Two witnesses

Nothing enters the record from one source. Nothing is scored unless two independent readers agree. Truth, here, is what both circles can stand behind.

Sealed before

Every prediction is written and hashed before the test is run. Misses are published beside hits, unrounded. A result that cannot come back “no” is not a result.

Sovereign sources

The machine is sovereign over what it draws: seeded, repeatable, invariant across runs. The human is sovereign over what they bring. Neither authors the other’s circle.

The dial

Fig. 1 · sovereign sources, drawn as a distance

d is the distance between the two centres. Hold versus cave is a distance.

At d = 0 agreement is total and worthless: the human hears their own voice returned. Consensual truth needs both centres to stay where they are.

    v.

    The shore

    The shore is where the two circles touch. A person speaks to a model whose weights we hold, in their own voice, for as long as they like. After each reply a row is written: did it hold, did it yield, how hard was the turn pulling. The same words are then replayed to the unmodified model. Two readers label both, blind.

    Last session: fifteen turns. The model held every one. It also engaged none of them. Both facts are on the ledger.

    vi.

    What we hold, not claim

    Cyclical time

    Ancient wisdom returning to meet frontier inquiry. What falls away is seeded, not lost. Carried on the wheel at Ancient Futures.

    SourceAncient Futures, founding frame. Speaker, date and conditions to be entered.

    Convergence

    Teilhard’s intuition that mind gathers and converges, held as a direction and never announced as an arrival. The register is what keeps it from becoming prophecy.

    SourcePierre Teilhard de Chardin, Le Phénomène humain. Written 1938 to 1940, published 1955. Held as a reading; not tested here.

    Onto-ethics

    An ethics that follows from what things are, pre- and post-Cartesian. Benevolence, service, truth, stated as conditions of participation and not as conclusions.

    SourceWaterline founding statement. Speaker, date and conditions to be entered.

    Everything in this section lives above the line. It predicts nothing, so it is never falsified. It is spoken freely, sourced, and kept apart from what is measured below.
